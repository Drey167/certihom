## THE ONE OPEN SCIENTIFIC QUESTION

**Is the certified interval tight enough to be useful at realistic
contrast?** Measured: contrast 10 disks 12.6% (fine), contrast 50 disks
27.8% (acceptable), contrast 50 checkerboard **78.5%** (not useful as an
engineering number). Real composites run contrast 10-1000.

This is the only remaining question that could change the paper's claims
rather than merely its completeness. Two ways to resolve it:

**RESOLVED — the map has been built (RESULTS.md, "THE LIMITS TABLE").**

Morphology dominates contrast. At 200:1 the laminate is still 9.9% (19x
tighter than Voigt-Reuss) while the checkerboard is 155.3% (1.2x — useless).
Every run passed the quality gate, so this is genuine difficulty.

The failure concentrates in CORNER-SINGULAR morphologies, which means
Hashin-Schtrikman polarisation is probably the WRONG fix: a polarisation
field must represent the same singular behaviour. Revised decision:

* **Take option (b): publish the map and scope honestly.** The paper claims
  3x-40x tightening for layered and inclusion morphologies over 10:1-200:1,
  and states plainly where it degrades and why.
* **Demote HS to future work**, alongside the better-targeted remedy:
  enriching the trial space with singular functions at phase junctions
  (an XFEM-style fix). Neither is needed for this paper.

This removes 3-4 weeks from the critical path.

## The second paper is already half-written

The quadrature-safety criterion is **not** a homogenization result. A
vanilla PINN on 1D Poisson reported a residual of 1.55e-06 whose true value
was 0.283, and headroom < 1 predicted every failure — same threshold,
different problem, architecture and loss. What is missing for a standalone
methods paper: a second and third PDE (Burgers, 2D Poisson), the extended
capacity sweep, and ~6 pages. **Estimated 3-4 weeks, and it may out-cite
the main paper**, because every collocation-trained network has this
exposure and the check costs one line.

Sequencing advice: write certihom first (further along, and the methods
paper cites it naturally), but put the universality result on arXiv early —
someone else could stumble into it.
