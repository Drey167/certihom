\title{Certified Amortized Homogenization: Guaranteed Two-Sided Bounds on
Effective Tensors from Operator-Learned Admissible Fields}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{abstract}
Computational homogenization returns effective properties without error
bars, while variational bounds return certified intervals but require a
separate solve for every microstructure. We remove that restriction. Primal
and dual trial fields are conditioned on the microstructure so that a
single trained operator emits certified two-sided L\"owner bounds for
microstructures it has never seen, in 19\,ms for scalar conductivity and
62\,ms for plane-strain elasticity. Because admissibility is enforced
architecturally, conditioning cannot invalidate the bounds: out-of-%
distribution inputs widen the certificate rather than falsifying it. On
unseen microstructures the intervals are two to four times narrower than
the Voigt--Reuss envelope, and they distinguish microstructures that
envelope provably cannot---a laminate and a checkerboard of identical phase
fraction receive certified widths of 18.3\% and 43.2\%. We extend the
construction to tensor-valued elasticity using Airy potentials certified on
$C^1$ Bogner--Fox--Schmit elements, and show that certified design at fixed
material budget raises guaranteed performance floors by 55.3\% and 36.5\%.
Finally, we give a computable criterion comparing a trained field's
spectral bandwidth against the quadrature Nyquist limit, which predicts
collocation overfitting before certification degrades, acts as a
label-free per-run quality gate, and prescribes the collocation density; we
show it also applies to physics-informed networks outside homogenization.
Certified widths and their limits are mapped across phase contrast and four
morphology classes, including the cases where the method degrades.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Certified two-sided bounds on effective properties have until now required
a separate solve for every microstructure, while amortized surrogates that
carry a guarantee have carried only one derived from phase fractions. We
have shown that the two can be combined: conditioning primal and dual trial
fields on the microstructure, in a manner that preserves conformity, yields
certified L\"owner intervals for unseen microstructures at inference cost,
two to four times narrower than the analytic envelope and sensitive to
geometry that the envelope cannot resolve. Because admissibility is
architectural rather than penalised, this transfer costs validity nothing:
an unfamiliar input widens the certificate instead of falsifying it, which
makes interval width an assumption-free indicator of competence.

Two further results seem to us to have consequences beyond the present
setting. Certifying the elastic lower bound requires stresses that remain
equilibrated after projection, which $C^1$ Bogner--Fox--Schmit
interpolation of an Airy potential supplies exactly; the same argument
should carry to any bound requiring an $H(\operatorname{div})$-conforming
dual. And the comparison between a trained field's spectral bandwidth and
the Nyquist limit of its quadrature rule predicts collocation overfitting
before it corrupts a certificate, gates individual runs without reference
data, and prescribes the collocation density---a diagnostic that applies to
collocation-trained physics networks generally, not only to bounded
problems.

The boundary of usefulness is mapped rather than asserted. Certificates
remain tight for layered, particulate, fibrous and polycrystalline
morphologies to substantial phase contrast, and degrade towards the
analytic envelope for corner-singular and bicontinuous morphologies above
roughly $50{:}1$. That degradation tracks the complexity of the exact
corrector and is, on the evidence of a matched-budget comparison,
independent of the physics; it is a limitation of approximating complex
corrector fields by smooth networks, and it is announced by the interval
width rather than hidden by it. Extending the construction to three
dimensions, and enriching the trial space where the corrector is singular,
are the natural next steps.
